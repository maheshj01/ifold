Your desktop folds with the lid.

iFold reads your MacBook's hinge sensor and bends the live desktop as the lid comes down — perspective, motion blur, a soft click as it springs back flat. Like a foldable, on the Mac you already have.

Download for Mac

iFold.dmg · 0.6 MB · macOS 14+ · Apple silicon MacBook with a lid sensor (M2 Air and later, 14"/16" Pro) · all releases

Free and open source. It isn't notarized yet, so macOS shows one warning on first launch — it takes a single click to get past, steps below.

Install in four steps

Two are the usual drag-and-drop. The other two are because this build is signed but not yet notarized by Apple — you'll see one warning, and you'll grant one permission.

Open the DMG, drag iFold to Applications

Then eject the disk image and launch iFold from Applications or Spotlight.

When macOS says it can't verify iFold, click Done

Not Move to Trash. This dialog appears for every app that isn't notarized, and clicking Done is what unlocks the next step.

“iFold.app” Not Opened

Apple could not verify “iFold.app” is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy.

Move to Trash Done

Allow it once

Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to the bottom to "iFold.app was blocked to protect your Mac" and click Open Anyway, then Open. You won't be asked again.

or, skip the dialog from Terminal
xattr -d com.apple.quarantine /Applications/iFold.app

Grant Screen Recording, then Relaunch

iFold draws a bent copy of your screen, so macOS asks for Screen Recording. Enable it under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording, then click Relaunch in the iFold window. Frames stay in GPU memory and are never saved or sent anywhere.

Why the warning?

Notarization is Apple scanning a build and vouching for it, and it's only available through the paid Developer Program. iFold is signed with a developer certificate but hasn't been through that yet, so macOS is cautious by default — it doesn't mean anything was found.

If you'd rather not trust a binary at all, build it from source in about a minute; the app you get is the same. Every release publishes a SHA-256 so you can check the download matches.

  • On device only Captured frames go straight from ScreenCaptureKit to the GPU. Nothing is encoded, written to disk, or uploaded — the app links no networking framework.
  • Dropped on sleep and lock The capture stream stops, and the overlay is torn down, the moment the Mac sleeps, the display sleeps, or the screen locks.
  • Only the lid sensor The hardware access is pinned to Apple's lid-angle sensor. iFold asks for no Accessibility, Input Monitoring, camera, or microphone permission.
  • Open source, MIT Read the code, file an issue, or send a fix.
